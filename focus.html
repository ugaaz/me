Areas of interest

Where the work concentrates.

Eight categories drawn from the research, teaching and public work of Guuleed Green. Each one is a door into the same question: how Somalia can manage a changing environment with evidence and fair institutions.

01

Environmental Governance

Environmental outcomes in Somalia are decided as much in offices, courts and local authorities as they are in the field. This line of work looks at the policies, mandates and accountability systems that govern land, water, biodiversity, waste and climate risk — from district practice to national frameworks — and at what it takes to make those rules real.

02

Climate Change & Resilience

The Horn of Africa is already living climate change: failed rains, flash floods, heat, and the movement of people and livestock under stress. The interest here is not climate as a slogan. It is resilience as public policy — early warning, adaptation, livelihoods, and the institutions that must hold when the weather does not.

03

Pollution & Waste

Solid waste, plastics, informal dumping and hazardous materials are reshaping urban and coastal environments. This work treats pollution as an environmental health and governance problem: who generates waste, who is exposed, and which systems can reduce harm without waiting for a perfect municipality.

04

Water & Wastewater

Water quality, scarcity, groundwater, rivers and wastewater sit at the centre of both daily life and national development. The scientific interest is in how water is measured, protected and allocated; the policy interest is in making that science usable for utilities, local government and communities.

05

Urban Environmental Management

Mogadishu and other Somali towns are growing faster than their environmental services. Urban environmental management looks at cities as systems — drainage, waste, air, green space, flooding and liveability — and at how planning can keep urban growth from becoming a permanent ecological debt.

06

Environmental Data & GIS

GIS, remote sensing and environmental data make changing landscapes visible. This work is about using maps and measurements to understand land cover, drought, settlement, water and development patterns — and about closing the data gap that still separates Somali decision-makers from the evidence they need.

07

Climate Communication

Evidence that cannot be explained will not change a budget, a by-law or a classroom. Climate communication here means translating research into public conversation: lectures, briefings, essays and teaching materials that reach citizens and policymakers without flattening the science.

08

Sustainable Development

Development that ignores ecosystems will fail; conservation that ignores poverty will not last. This category holds the practical question of Guuleed Green: which pathways can restore land and water, reduce poverty, and remain honest about the environmental limits of Somalia’s recovery.
